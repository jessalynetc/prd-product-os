# Intake and Product Framing

## Minimum intake model

Reconstruct or gather: idea, why now, target user, problem/job, desired outcome, build type, build budget, existing assets, hard constraints, and biggest uncertainty.

Do not present this as a ten-question form. Pre-fill from evidence, label assumptions, and ask only questions whose answers would materially affect direction, P0, UX, architecture, risk, success, or implementation.

## Interview pattern

1. Summarize what is already known.
2. Propose the most likely framing.
3. Explain the important reasoning or trade-off.
4. Ask one decision-oriented question.
5. After the answer, update confirmed facts, assumptions, decisions, gaps, and next question.

## Framing output

- **Target user:** who experiences the problem.
- **Problem:** what is difficult, confusing, risky, expensive, inaccessible, or impossible.
- **Context:** when the problem occurs.
- **Evidence:** what supports the belief; do not invent it.
- **Current alternative:** how the user handles it now.
- **Desired outcome:** what becomes meaningfully better.
- **Product thesis:** “We are building [product] for [user] so they can [outcome]. Unlike [alternative], it [differentiator].”
- **Principles:** no more than three project-specific trade-off filters; avoid “intuitive,” “beautiful,” and similar generic claims.

## Success

Define user outcome, product outcome, and quality outcome. Use numbers only when credible baselines exist. Otherwise define the signal, measurement method, and when to establish a baseline.

## Adaptive prompts

- **AI product:** role, context, allowed actions, confirmation boundaries, grounding, uncertainty, failure, human review, evaluation.
- **Interactive web:** storytelling vs exploration vs task, scroll/keyboard/touch mapping, reduced-motion fallback, desktop/mobile model.
- **High-risk product:** money/PII, permissions, compliance, irreversible actions, recovery.
